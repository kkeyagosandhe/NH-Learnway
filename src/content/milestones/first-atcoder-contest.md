  unlocks: Working under a clock
  confidence: >-
    the clock stops rattling you — you can read a problem carefully and code it
    calmly even though it counts.
problem: >-
  Untimed practice and a live contest are different sports. A contest adds a clock,
  unfamiliar problems, and the small adrenaline of it counting. That is a muscle
  you can only train by doing it. AtCoder Beginner Contests (ABCs) are the gentlest
  place to start: clean statements, no tricks for the sake of tricks, and problems
  that ramp smoothly from very easy.
analogy: >-
  Your first parkrun or 5k. You are not there to beat the person who trains full
  time. You are there to prove you can pin on a number, start when the gun goes,
  and finish a couple of kilometres. Everyone around you was a first-timer once,
  and finishing at all is the whole win.
intuition: >-
  Go in aiming to solve A and B — nothing more. Read those first two carefully,
  because in a friendly contest the difficulty is honest: A is meant to be quick, B
  a small step up. Partial success is success. The rating is noise this early; the
  rep is the point.
prereqs:
  - Some CSES Introductory problems under your belt.
  - Fast input/output set up (the sys.stdin line from the last milestone).
  - A quiet 100-minute block where you will not be interrupted.
walkthrough:
  - Register for the next ABC a day ahead so contest day has zero setup friction.
  - When it starts, open problem A, solve it, and test on the sample cases before submitting.
  - Move to B. Read it twice; a careful read beats a fast wrong guess.
  - Always run your code on the provided samples before you submit — a failed sample is a free catch.
  - When the contest ends, upsolve problem C using the editorial. The learning is in the upsolve, not the scoreboard.
build: >-
  The contest is the build. Afterwards, write one line in your notes: which
  problems you solved, and where you got stuck. That log is how you will see
  yourself improve over the next few contests.
interview: >-
  Not directly an interview activity, but the composure it builds transfers.
  Interviews are timed problem-solving in front of someone; a few contests make
  that feel routine instead of frightening.
