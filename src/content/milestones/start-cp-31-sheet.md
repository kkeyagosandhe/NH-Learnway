problem: >-
  Practising on random contest problems is noisy — some are far too easy, some
  wildly too hard, and neither teaches much. What actually builds a competitive
  programmer is a graded ladder: problems sorted by difficulty so each one is a
  small, deliberate stretch above the last. The CP-31 sheet is that ladder, starting
  at rating 800.
analogy: >-
  A climbing wall with colour-graded routes. You do not fling yourself at the
  expert overhang on day one. You clear the beginner grade until it feels easy,
  then move up one colour. Each route is just hard enough to make you reach, never
  so hard you fall off and quit.
intuition: >-
  The sheet is ordered by rating band. Work a band until it stops feeling like a
  fight, then step up. The goal is consistent, slightly-uncomfortable reps, not
  heroics. Progress in competitive programming is almost entirely a function of
  doing problems a notch above your current comfort, over and over, for months.
prereqs:
  - A few contests done, so you know the format.
  - Honesty about your current level, so you start at the right rung.
  - A way to log which problems you solved and which you had to look up.
walkthrough:
  - Start at the 800 band even if it feels easy — a couple of clean warm-up solves builds rhythm.
  - Do a few problems per week from your current band; quality of understanding beats quantity.
  - When you can solve most of a band without help, climb to the next rating up.
  - Log every problem you could not solve alone and revisit it a week later. Repeat-until-independent is the whole method.
build: >-
  The sheet is the practice. Pair it with your contests: contests reveal your weak
  spots, the ladder lets you drill them deliberately between rounds.
interview: >-
  Same story as the contests — indirect but compounding. A climbing rating is
  evidence to yourself, more than to anyone else, that the quiet daily reps are
  turning into real ability.

    green checks while your actual level stops moving.
