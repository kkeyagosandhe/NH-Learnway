title: Write your first pattern note
do: >-
  In your own words, write two sentences: what pattern you used, and when you
  would reuse it.
why: The bridge from I-built-it to I-can-explain-it-in-an-interview.
meta:
  interview: 3
  realWorld: 3
  time: fifteen minutes
  confidence: >-
    you can explain a pattern out loud, from memory, without looking at the code —
    because you once wrote it down in your own words.
problem: >-
  There is a canyon between solving a problem and being able to explain it. You can
  make the code work and still go blank when someone asks "so why a hash map
  there?" The fix is almost embarrassingly simple and almost nobody does it: write,
  in your own words, what pattern you used and when you would reach for it again.
  Writing forces the fuzzy "I sort of get it" into words, and words are what an
  interview asks for.
analogy: >-
  A naturalist's field notebook. Sketching the bird you saw — badly, in pencil —
  makes you actually look at it: the beak, the markings, the posture. You retain it
  in a way that snapping a photo never gives you. Your pattern note is that sketch.
  The point is not a pretty entry; it is the looking that writing forces.
intuition: >-
  This is the testing effect and the Feynman idea rolled together: retrieving and
  explaining a thing cements it far better than re-reading it, and the moment your
  explanation stalls is the moment you have found the gap you did not know you had.
  Two honest sentences beat a folder of copied solutions. Start a running document —
  one short note per pattern — and it becomes your personal interview-prep bible.
real:
  - Engineers keep "brag docs" and decision logs for the same reason — writing clarifies thinking.
  - Teaching or explaining code in review is how understanding gets tested on the job.
  - Your note file becomes the thing you skim the night before an interview.
build: >-
  Open a plain document titled "Patterns." Write your first entry now, for a
  pattern you have already used — hash map, two pointers, sliding window, whatever
  is freshest. Two sentences: (1) what it does, (2) the signal that tells you to
  reach for it. Add one entry every time a pattern finally clicks.
reflect: >-
  Write your first pattern note right here, then copy it into your document: what
  pattern, and when would you reuse it?
