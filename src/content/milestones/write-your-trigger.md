title: Write your trigger
do: >-
  Write one sentence: "After I ___ (something I already do every day), I will
  open NeetCode and do one problem." Put it where you will see it.
why: This sentence is the on-switch for the whole habit.
problem: >-
  New habits do not fail because people are lazy. They fail because the habit has
  no cue — nothing in the day reliably says "now." Willpower is a terrible cue; it
  shows up late and leaves early. You need to bolt the new thing onto something
  that already happens without fail, so it rides along for free.
analogy: >-
  It is like hanging your umbrella on the front-door handle instead of in the
  closet. You do not have to remember the umbrella — remembering to leave the
  house is enough, and the umbrella is right there in the way. Your existing daily
  habit (morning coffee, closing the laptop at work) is the door handle. The one
  problem a day is the umbrella hanging on it.
intuition: >-
  Researchers call this an implementation intention, or habit stacking: "After
  [existing habit], I will [tiny new habit]." The existing habit is already
  automatic, so it drags the new one along until the new one becomes automatic
  too. Keep the new part almost insultingly small — one problem, not one hour — so
  there is never a reason to skip it.
reflect: "Write your sentence here, exactly as it will read on the sticky note."
